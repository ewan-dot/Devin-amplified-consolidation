Before the AI is allowed to write a file, modify a database node, or proceed with a search, it must run the lookup through a deterministic filter that guarantees a confidence score of $\ge 0.95$. If the certainty is lower, the system halts.

Here is the mathematical and linguistic structure of how this operates:

---

### 1. Define Everything Prior to Writing (Agreed Filenames & Meanings)
The system operates on a closed, authoritative dictionary:
*   Every technical filename, expert identifier, and semantic domain is declared in [glasses_registry.json](file:///Users/ewansair/ingestion-to-research-pipe/perplexity-inbox/harness/glasses_registry.json) and [ESTATE-TAXONOMY.md](file:///Users/ewansair/ingestion-to-research-pipe/perplexity-inbox/ESTATE-TAXONOMY.md).
*   If a name does not exist in this canonical index, the system cannot guess or invent a new one. It must treat the input as "out-of-bounds" until formally defined.

---

### 2. Clustering Around the Canonical Centers
Instead of letting typos and grammatical variants scatter across the database, we treat the canonical target as a **center of gravity** and pull everything toward it:

```
        [banadas] (Typo) ───┐
                            │ (Edit Distance)
        [banada]  (Typo) ───┼───► [BANANAS] (Canonical Center)
                            │
        [banad]   (Typo) ───┘
```

1.  **Spelling Clustering (Levenshtein):** All fat-finger typos (e.g. `cve`, `velum`, `banadas`) are mathematically measured and mapped to their nearest canonical centers (`cove`, `vellum`, `bananas`).
2.  **Grammatical Clustering (Stems):** All grammatical variations of a word (e.g., `pricing`, `priced`, `price`) are stemmed and grouped around the single agreed-upon domain node (`pricing`).

---

### 3. The 0.95 Certainty Gate (Taguchi & Russian Probability)
We use a combination of **Taguchi deviation metrics ($F12$)** and **probability bounds (Markov Absorption $F14$)** to calculate the final confidence score:

*   **The Threshold:** The lookup match must satisfy:
    $$\text{Confidence} \ge 0.95$$
*   **If Confidence is $\ge 0.95$:** The spelling or grammatical autocorrect is applied automatically (e.g. `Seth Golden` $\rightarrow$ `Seth Godin`). The search executes using the canonical target.
*   **If Confidence is $< 0.95$:** The system declares that the path is too fuzzy. The Taguchi loss function calculates that the risk of a "precise wrongness" error is too high, and the script halts to prevent the AI from creating junk.

This is how we let the AI search for what it is *actually* looking for, using strict math to bridge the gap between human imprecision and machine precision.
