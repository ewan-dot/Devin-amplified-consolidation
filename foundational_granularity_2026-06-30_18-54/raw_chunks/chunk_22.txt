### ASSISTANT (Session 6ff55ae9)
Yes, there is clear, mature prior art that directly establishes the foundations of this design. We stand on the shoulders of several specific giants:

1.  **Rabin Fingerprints (1981):** Michael O. Rabin's paper *"Fingerprinting by Random Polynomials"* established the rolling hash mathematics that enables sliding window byte checks in constant time.
2.  **Content-Defined Chunking (2001):** Muthitacharoen et al. (MIT) in *"A Low-bandwidth Network File System (LBFS)"* pioneered using Rabin Fingerprints to split file streams into variable-sized chunks dynamically, achieving massive deduplication and network savings. FastCDC (Xia et al. 2016) later optimized this logic for sub-millisecond execution.
3.  **Plan 9 Venti (2002):** Sean Quinlan & Sean Dorward (Bell Labs) built *"Venti: a new approach to writing to archive"* which established the definitive content-addressed storage (CAS) architecture using cryptographic hashes as immutable data blocks.
4.  **EMC Centera (2003):** The first commercial enterprise CAS implementation, introducing content-derived hash addresses as permanent digital claim checks.
5.  **W3C RDF (1999):** Standardized by the W3C as a schema-independent, universal graph-structured model representing metadata as flat Subject-Predicate-Object triples, matching our relational mapping layer.
