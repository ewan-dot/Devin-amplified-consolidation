## What Can Be Deterministically Deduced from Text — Published Academic Research

Every item below is computable from text without a language model in the detection loop.

---

### 1. Epistemic Stance — Certainty vs. Doubt
**Source**: Warchal (2010), Hyland (2005), Vold (2006) — extensive corpus linguistics literature

Epistemic modality markers divide into two opposing poles:

**Certainty boosters**: *must, will, certainly, definitely, clearly, obviously, undoubtedly, for sure, no doubt*

**Hedges / uncertainty markers**: *might, may, could, possibly, perhaps, apparently, seem, suggest, indicate, tend to, it is possible that, I think, I believe*

These form finite, annotated lexicons. Score per turn: ratio of boosters to hedges gives a **confidence index**. Fully deterministic — lookup table against token stream.

---

### 2. Commitment Level — Assertive vs. Tentative
**Source**: Speech Act Theory (Austin 1962, Searle 1969), Pragmatics of Commitment literature

- **High commitment**: declarative sentences, active voice, present tense, no modal softeners
- **Low commitment**: conditional framing (*if*, *were to*), passive constructions, future tense with hedges, rhetorical questions

Detectable via POS tags + dependency parse. No model needed.

---

### 3. Deception Signals
**Source**: ACL/CACM literature (Newman et al. 2003, Hancock et al.), LIWC research

Deception in text produces measurable patterns:

- **Fewer first-person singular pronouns** (*I*, *me*, *my*) — distancing from the lie
- **More negative emotion words**
- **Fewer exclusive words** (*but*, *except*, *without*) — less complex cognitive processing
- **More motion verbs** — action substituted for thought
- **Shorter, simpler sentences** — cognitive load of maintaining a lie reduces elaboration

All computable via LIWC lexicon or equivalent word lists. Pure frequency counting.

---

### 4. Emotional Valence, Arousal, Dominance (VAD)
**Source**: Mohammad (2025), NRC VAD Lexicon v2 — 25,000 words + 10,000 multiword expressions, human-rated, freely available. Split-half reliability r=0.99 (valence), r=0.98 (arousal), r=0.96 (dominance)

Three independent dimensions scored per text:

- **Valence**: positive↔negative
- **Arousal**: excited/active↔calm/passive
- **Dominance**: powerful/in-control↔submissive/deferential

Lookup table against the NRC VAD Lexicon. Average across tokens. Fully deterministic. No model.

**Application for your system**: dominance drop between turns signals deference. Arousal spike signals urgency or stress. Both are detectable without AI.

---

### 5. Power and Dominance in Discourse
**Source**: Critical Discourse Analysis (Fairclough), Sociolinguistics (Giles)

Markers of power/dominance in text:

- **Interruption patterns** (in transcripts) — dominant party interrupts more
- **Question asking** — dominant party asks more questions, receives more answers
- **Topic initiation** — who introduces new topics
- **Directive speech acts** — commands vs. requests (*do this* vs. *could you perhaps*)
- **Hedging asymmetry** — subordinate party hedges more

All computable from turn structure + speech act classification. The hedge asymmetry alone is a single ratio calculation.

---

### 6. Cognitive Load / Text Complexity
**Source**: Flesch (1948), Flesch-Kincaid (1975), Gunning Fog, SMOG — all validated against reading comprehension data

Metrics computable from raw text:
- Average sentence length (words)
- Average syllable count per word
- Ratio of polysyllabic words
- Type-token ratio (lexical diversity)
- Subordinate clause depth (via parse tree)

**Flesch Reading Ease**: 206.835 − (1.015 × avg sentence length) − (84.6 × avg syllables per word). Pure arithmetic.

**Application**: detect when a turn's complexity spikes — could indicate obfuscation, uncertainty, or the speaker reaching the edge of their knowledge.

---

### 7. Topic Shift
**Source**: TextTiling (Hearst 1997), discourse segmentation literature

Lexical cohesion drops measurably at topic boundaries. Sliding window over term frequency vectors — cosine similarity between adjacent windows. When similarity drops below threshold, topic has shifted.

Pure linear algebra. Detectable in real time as conversation proceeds.

---

### 8. Urgency / Priority Signals
**Source**: Speech Acts literature, ACL 2022 urgency detection paper

- **Imperative constructions** (*do X now*, *this must be done*)
- **Temporal markers** (*immediately*, *urgent*, *deadline*, *by tomorrow*)
- **Negative consequence framing** (*or else*, *otherwise*, *if not*)
- **Exclamatory syntax**

Finite lexicon + syntactic pattern. Regex-level detection.

---

### 9. Convergence / Divergence (Communication Accommodation Theory)
**Source**: Giles (1973, 2016), CAT literature

As described earlier — vocabulary overlap between speakers across a sliding window. Measurable as cosine similarity drift between party A and party B term frequency vectors over time.

Divergence (parties moving apart linguistically) is as detectable as convergence.

---

### 10. Retraction / Epistemic Instability
**Source**: NLP retraction literature, Commitment markers in linguistics

Signs a speaker is walking something back:

- **Contrastive connectives after assertion**: *but*, *however*, *although*, *that said*, *on the other hand* — within same turn as a prior strong claim
- **Downgraders**: *actually*, *well*, *I mean*, *to be fair*, *in a sense*
- **Reformulation markers**: *or rather*, *what I mean is*, *let me rephrase*

All finite lexicon items. The pattern is: strong epistemic marker followed within N tokens by a contrastive connective. Pure sequence matching.

---

### 11. Politeness / Face-Threat Mitigation
**Source**: Brown & Levinson (1987), Politeness Theory — the most cited pragmatics framework

Face-threatening acts (criticism, disagreement, requests) are systematically softened with:

- **Hedges** (*perhaps*, *possibly*)
- **Indirectness** (*could you possibly...* instead of *do this*)
- **Solidarity markers** (*we*, *us*, *our* instead of *you*)
- **Appreciation tokens** (*that's a great point, but...*)

The presence of appreciation immediately before a contradictory statement is a detectable pattern — it's the softening move before a face-threat. Computable.

## Seven Deterministic Linguistic Detectors

**Ambiguity**
Lexical: multiple senses per word, unresolved pronoun referents, quantifier scope. Syntactic: attachment ambiguity detectable via parse tree branching factor. Vagueness: hedged noun phrases without anchoring referent. All computable — dependency parse + WordNet sense count per token.

**Metaphor**
MIP (Metaphor Identification Procedure, Pragglejaz Group 2007) — compare contextual meaning to basic/literal meaning of each word. If they conflict and contextual meaning is understandable via the literal, it's metaphor. Rule-based pipeline exists. Flags abstraction being used where concreteness is needed — risk signal for drift from grounded reasoning.

**Certainty**
Booster/hedge ratio. Lexicon lookup. Already covered — keep it.

**Manipulation**
Distinct from persuasion. Linguistic markers: false urgency (*you must decide now*), false scarcity framing, social proof fabrication (*everyone agrees*), emotional arousal without factual content (high VAD arousal + low propositional density). Pattern match + VAD score. Detectable.

**Agreement**
Already covered. The risk signal.

**Tangent / Topic Shift**
TextTiling — cosine similarity drop between adjacent turn windows. Flags when conversation has forked from the goal. The question is whether the fork was intentional or drift.

**Relevance to Goal**
Sperber & Wilson Relevance Theory (1986) — relevance is measurable as cognitive effect per processing effort. Practically: semantic similarity between current utterance and a stored goal statement. Cosine similarity against a goal embedding. Drops below threshold = flag.

### ASSISTANT (Turn 59)
Interesting addition. In text — no audio — phonemes are still deterministically accessible via **grapheme-to-phoneme (G2P) conversion**. Fully rule-based for English, deterministic dictionaries exist (CMU Pronouncing Dictionary, 134,000 entries).

What phoneme patterns tell you from text:

**Prosodic stress patterns** — which syllables carry stress, detectable from word structure. Stress shift signals emphasis change.

**Phonaesthetics** — certain phoneme clusters carry consistent meaning associations across languages (Sapir 1929, confirmed repeatedly). Plosives (p, b, t, d, k, g) = hard, decisive, forceful. Fricatives (f, s, sh) = soft, uncertain, evasive. Detectable in word choice patterns — someone consistently choosing plosive-heavy vocabulary is linguistically projecting force regardless of semantic content.

**Alliteration and assonance** — repetition of phoneme patterns is a persuasion and rhetoric marker. Detectable via G2P + frequency count. High alliteration = rhetorical mode, not analytical mode.

**Phoneme frequency anomaly** — statistically unusual phoneme distribution in a passage signals non-native register, formulaic language, or copied/templated text.

All computable: text → G2P → phoneme sequence → statistical analysis.

The question is what you want phonemes *for* in this system. Rhetoric detection? Register shift? Or something more specific?
