## Agreement Detection — Cross-Disciplinary Synthesis

This is genuinely rich territory. Five distinct academic traditions all have something to say, and they converge on the same problem from different angles.

---

### 1. Conversation Analysis — the structural definition

Sacks, Schegloff & Jefferson (1974) — the founding framework. Agreement is a **preferred second pair part** in an assertion/agreement adjacency pair. The key empirical finding:

- **Preferred responses** (agreement) are fast, unmarked, no hedging
- **Dispreferred responses** (disagreement) are delayed, hedged, prefaced with an account

This gives you the **structural signal**: prompt, unhedged uptake of the prior assertion = agreement. Delay + "well..." + qualification = disagreement even if the surface content sounds agreeable.

Critically — **the absence of challenge is diagnostic**. In CA, silence after an assertion already signals a dispreferred response is coming. The structure of the exchange is as informative as its content.

**Direct application**: if neither party pauses, hedges, or challenges before converging — that's the structural signature of either genuine agreement or acquiescence. The structure alone can't distinguish them.

---

### 2. Speech Act Theory — Austin & Searle

Agreement is a **commissive** — it commits the speaker to a proposition. But Searle's taxonomy distinguishes:

- **Assertives**: claiming something is true
- **Expressives**: expressing a psychological state (including approval)

The problem: "you're absolutely right" is an **expressive**, not an assertive. It commits the speaker to a feeling, not to the truth of the proposition. Surface agreement tokens are often expressives masquerading as assertives.

**Direct application**: the hook needs to distinguish assertive agreement ("yes, that mechanism is correct because X") from expressive agreement ("exactly, great point"). Only assertive agreement with stated reasons carries epistemic weight.

---

### 3. Grice's Cooperative Principle — the implicature problem

Grice's maxim of **Quality** (don't say what you believe to be false) and **Quantity** (say enough, not too much) together predict that genuine agreement should be brief and informative. But the maxim of **Manner** (be clear) conflicts with face-saving behaviour.

The deep problem Grice identifies: **conversational implicature** means agreement can be communicated without being stated, and disagreement can be concealed behind apparent agreement. The surface form is systematically misleading.

---

### 4. Communication Accommodation Theory — Giles

**Convergence** — matching the other party's vocabulary, rhythm, framing — is a measurable signal. When two parties are converging linguistically (using each other's terms, mirroring sentence structures), that's a detectable pattern.

But CAT shows convergence is driven by **desire for social approval**, not necessarily epistemic alignment. Two people can converge linguistically while still holding incompatible beliefs. Convergence is necessary but not sufficient for genuine agreement.

**Direct application**: linguistic convergence (shared vocabulary, mirrored framing) is detectable computationally. It's a necessary condition for genuine agreement, not a sufficient one.

---

### 5. Negotiation Theory — the behavioural markers

Engel et al. (2026, Hamburg/CMU, *Negotiation and Conflict Management Research*) — 22,880 coded verbal behaviours from 40 face-to-face negotiation sessions:

- **Apologies correlate with agreements**
- **Procedural suggestions correlate with non-agreements** (still trying to restructure rather than settle)
- The path to agreement follows detectable **sequential patterns** in the interaction stream — it's not a single moment, it's a trajectory

This is the most operationally useful finding: **agreement has a temporal signature**, not just a point-in-time marker. You're looking for a trajectory of convergence, not a single token.

---

### 6. The Acquiescence Problem — Psychology

Acquiescence bias (the "yea-saying" tendency) is well documented in psychology. The critical distinction from genuine agreement:

- **Genuine agreement**: preceded by processing, possibly preceded by challenge, followed by elaboration that adds new content
- **Acquiescence**: fast, content-free, no elaboration, no evidence of having processed the prior claim

Sycophancy in LLMs (Cheng et al., Stanford 2025) is the acquiescence pattern made systematic by training. But the underlying distinction is a human one — psychologists have studied it in survey responses, authority compliance, and groupthink for decades.

---

### What this means for the hook

The research across six disciplines converges on **one finding**: you cannot detect genuine agreement from a single utterance. You need to look at:

| Signal | What it detects | Discipline |
|---|---|---|
| Speed of response | Preferred vs. dispreferred structure | CA |
| Presence of prior challenge | Whether agreement was earned | CA / Negotiation |
| Assertive vs. expressive form | Epistemic vs. social agreement | Speech Act Theory |
| Linguistic convergence trajectory | Shared framing developing over time | CAT |
| Elaboration with new content | Processing vs. reflection | Psychology |
| Behavioural sequence pattern | Agreement trajectory vs. agreement token | Negotiation |

**The hook trigger is not a word. It's a pattern across the exchange.** Specifically: fast uptake + no prior challenge + expressive form + linguistic mirroring + no new elaborative content = high-risk agreement requiring adversarial research.

That's detectable. It's not simple, but every component of it has academic grounding across multiple independent disciplines.

Assertive agreement includes a proposition with a truth claim — detectable by the presence of a subordinate clause following the agreement token. Parser-based, not model-based.

**Linguistic convergence trajectory** — cosine similarity between vocabulary sets across turns. Sliding window. If party B's term frequency distribution is moving toward party A's over N turns, that's convergence. Pure linear algebra. NumPy.

**Elaboration with new content** — information gain. Does the agreeing turn introduce new named entities, new predicates, new propositions — or does it restate what was just said? Computable via overlap coefficient between turns. If the agreement turn shares >80% of its semantic content with the prior turn, it's a mirror, not a contribution.

**Behavioural sequence pattern** — the negotiation literature's lag sequential analysis is a deterministic statistical method. It counts transition probabilities between coded behaviour types across the interaction stream. Fully implementable.

```

  → tokenise + parse (spaCy or similar, deterministic)
```

No model in the detection loop. The model is only involved in the adversarial research that fires *after* the hook triggers.

That's the point — you use deterministic code to decide *when* to question, and AI to do the questioning. The watchdog doesn't need to be clever. It needs to be consistent.

## What Can Be Deterministically Deduced from Text — Published Academic Research

Every item below is computable from text without a language model in the detection loop.

---

### 1. Epistemic Stance — Certainty vs. Doubt
**Source**: Warchal (2010), Hyland (2005), Vold (2006) — extensive corpus linguistics literature

Epistemic modality markers divide into two opposing poles:

**Certainty boosters**: *must, will, certainly, definitely, clearly, obviously, undoubtedly, for sure, no doubt*

**Hedges / uncertainty markers**: *might, may, could, possibly, perhaps, apparently, seem, suggest, indicate, tend to, it is possible that, I think, I believe*

These form finite, annotated lexicons. Score per turn: ratio of boosters to hedges gives a **confidence index**. Fully deterministic — lookup table against token stream.

---

### 2. Commitment Level — Assertive vs. Tentative
**Source**: Speech Act Theory (Austin 1962, Searle 1969), Pragmatics of Commitment literature

- **High commitment**: declarative sentences, active voice, present tense, no modal softeners
- **Low commitment**: conditional framing (*if*, *were to*), passive constructions, future tense with hedges, rhetorical questions

Detectable via POS tags + dependency parse. No model needed.

---

### 3. Deception Signals
**Source**: ACL/CACM literature (Newman et al. 2003, Hancock et al.), LIWC research

Deception in text produces measurable patterns:

- **Fewer first-person singular pronouns** (*I*, *me*, *my*) — distancing from the lie
- **More negative emotion words**
- **Fewer exclusive words** (*but*, *except*, *without*) — less complex cognitive processing
- **More motion verbs** — action substituted for thought
- **Shorter, simpler sentences** — cognitive load of maintaining a lie reduces elaboration

All computable via LIWC lexicon or equivalent word lists. Pure frequency counting.

---

### 4. Emotional Valence, Arousal, Dominance (VAD)
**Source**: Mohammad (2025), NRC VAD Lexicon v2 — 25,000 words + 10,000 multiword expressions, human-rated, freely available. Split-half reliability r=0.99 (valence), r=0.98 (arousal), r=0.96 (dominance)

Three independent dimensions scored per text:

- **Valence**: positive↔negative
- **Arousal**: excited/active↔calm/passive
- **Dominance**: powerful/in-control↔submissive/deferential

Lookup table against the NRC VAD Lexicon. Average across tokens. Fully deterministic. No model.

**Application for your system**: dominance drop between turns signals deference. Arousal spike signals urgency or stress. Both are detectable without AI.

---

### 5. Power and Dominance in Discourse
**Source**: Critical Discourse Analysis (Fairclough), Sociolinguistics (Giles)

Markers of power/dominance in text:

- **Interruption patterns** (in transcripts) — dominant party interrupts more
- **Question asking** — dominant party asks more questions, receives more answers
- **Topic initiation** — who introduces new topics
- **Directive speech acts** — commands vs. requests (*do this* vs. *could you perhaps*)
- **Hedging asymmetry** — subordinate party hedges more

All computable from turn structure + speech act classification. The hedge asymmetry alone is a single ratio calculation.

---

### 6. Cognitive Load / Text Complexity
**Source**: Flesch (1948), Flesch-Kincaid (1975), Gunning Fog, SMOG — all validated against reading comprehension data

Metrics computable from raw text:
- Average sentence length (words)
- Average syllable count per word
- Ratio of polysyllabic words
- Type-token ratio (lexical diversity)
- Subordinate clause depth (via parse tree)

**Flesch Reading Ease**: 206.835 − (1.015 × avg sentence length) − (84.6 × avg syllables per word). Pure arithmetic.

**Application**: detect when a turn's complexity spikes — could indicate obfuscation, uncertainty, or the speaker reaching the edge of their knowledge.

---

### 7. Topic Shift
**Source**: TextTiling (Hearst 1997), discourse segmentation literature

Lexical cohesion drops measurably at topic boundaries. Sliding window over term frequency vectors — cosine similarity between adjacent windows. When similarity drops below threshold, topic has shifted.

Pure linear algebra. Detectable in real time as conversation proceeds.

---

### 8. Urgency / Priority Signals
**Source**: Speech Acts literature, ACL 2022 urgency detection paper

- **Imperative constructions** (*do X now*, *this must be done*)
- **Temporal markers** (*immediately*, *urgent*, *deadline*, *by tomorrow*)
- **Negative consequence framing** (*or else*, *otherwise*, *if not*)
- **Exclamatory syntax**

Finite lexicon + syntactic pattern. Regex-level detection.

---

### 9. Convergence / Divergence (Communication Accommodation Theory)
**Source**: Giles (1973, 2016), CAT literature

As described earlier — vocabulary overlap between speakers across a sliding window. Measurable as cosine similarity drift between party A and party B term frequency vectors over time.

Divergence (parties moving apart linguistically) is as detectable as convergence.

---

### 10. Retraction / Epistemic Instability
**Source**: NLP retraction literature, Commitment markers in linguistics

Signs a speaker is walking something back:

- **Contrastive connectives after assertion**: *but*, *however*, *although*, *that said*, *on the other hand* — within same turn as a prior strong claim
- **Downgraders**: *actually*, *well*, *I mean*, *to be fair*, *in a sense*
- **Reformulation markers**: *or rather*, *what I mean is*, *let me rephrase*

All finite lexicon items. The pattern is: strong epistemic marker followed within N tokens by a contrastive connective. Pure sequence matching.

---

### 11. Politeness / Face-Threat Mitigation
**Source**: Brown & Levinson (1987), Politeness Theory — the most cited pragmatics framework

Face-threatening acts (criticism, disagreement, requests) are systematically softened with:

- **Hedges** (*perhaps*, *possibly*)
- **Indirectness** (*could you possibly...* instead of *do this*)
- **Solidarity markers** (*we*, *us*, *our* instead of *you*)
- **Appreciation tokens** (*that's a great point, but...*)

The presence of appreciation immediately before a contradictory statement is a detectable pattern — it's the softening move before a face-threat. Computable.
