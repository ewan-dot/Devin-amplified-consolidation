On Dalio — the Bridgewater decision criteria before implementation. The four are typically:

1. **How much** — magnitude of expected impact
2. **How complex** — implementation burden, interdependencies
3. **Proven benefit** — evidence base, track record
4. **How confident** — conviction level in the assessment, or sometimes stated as **reversibility** — can you undo it if wrong

The fourth varies by source. Some accounts say **probability of success**, others say **cost of being wrong**. The reversibility framing is the most useful here — it maps directly to your rubric. These four map cleanly onto rubric labels on the graph nodes:

```

```

### ASSISTANT (Turn 117)
Kolmogorov.

Kolmogorov complexity — the minimum description length of a string. The closer two things are in their minimum description, the more structurally similar they are. Distance between domains is measurable as the difference in their Kolmogorov complexity profiles.

And Kolmogorov distance is already used in cross-domain similarity — Normalized Compression Distance (NCD) is the practical implementation. You don't need the theoretical Kolmogorov complexity (uncomputable) — you approximate it with a compressor. Same input, compress it, measure the length. Two things that compress together well are structurally similar.

That's the pre-flight check. Before the lens rotates and attempts a cross-domain retrieval, compute the NCD between the source domain and the target domain. If it's above threshold — the domains are too far apart, the hop won't hold, flag it.

Deterministic. Computable. No model in the loop.

And it scales across dimensional distance too. Bacterial vs tectonic would have high NCD — their minimum descriptions are structurally different. Cash flow vs staffing in SMB context — low NCD. The shapes compress together. The retrieval is worth attempting.

This needs research to confirm it's been applied to knowledge graph retrieval specifically — but the theoretical grounding is solid. Kolmogorov/NCD is the Russian math you're pointing at.
