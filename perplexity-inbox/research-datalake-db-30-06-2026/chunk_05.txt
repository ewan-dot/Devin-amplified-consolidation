1. Purpose of the Closed Ingestion-to-Research-to-Brain Loop
The core purpose of this closed loop is to establish a self-compounding, automated knowledge and logic engine for the company. It moves the system from static libraries to an active production substrate.

Specifically, the pipeline automates the transition from raw inputs to verified, structured intelligence:

Raw Ingestion (Fetcher/Inbox): Receives raw unstructured inputs (emails, transcripts, voice logs, external feeds).
Research Pipe (Beast): Decomposes topics into raw probes and executes the five-stage search shape (fan-out → brutal demote → dual-POV precision → neighborhood expansion → Swanson combine) to gather external and academic evidence.
Inbox Staging (W1 Weld): Generates structured markdown briefs stamped with run IDs, packet hashes, and epistemic tier ratings (e.g., STRUCTURED, MEASURED), and POSTs them to the local ingest boundary.
Brain Ingest (W2 Weld/Temporal): A Temporal workflow runs on a 10-minute schedule, picking up these briefs, generating manifests, and indexing them as vectors.
The Two-Lane Split (Why this loop is unique)
The loop explicitly separates incoming material into two distinct pathways at the database gate:

Information & Narrative Lane: Facts, findings, and context that are free to enter the database to enrich future RAG retrieval and content generation.
Methodology & Logic Lane: Rules, decision procedures, and parameters that govern how the agents actually act. This lane is tightly gated. A new methodology enters as a candidate and is only promoted to the active production floor if it deterministicially passes evaluation criteria (R1/R2 rubrics), proving it beats the incumbent.
2. Plan with Regard to the Existing DB in the Brain
We treat the existing database (over 2.6M vectors stored in PostgreSQL and HNSW-indexed) as the canonical foundation of the estate's memory. We do not wipe or replace it.

Our plan for maintaining, updating, and interacting with this DB is structured around four rules:

Idempotent Upsert and Deduplication:
The ingestion process matches new items against existing records using content hashing and SHA-256 signatures to avoid polluting the database with duplicate evidence.
Strict Epistemic Curation (R1 Entry Gate):
Staged packets must pass Rubric R1 inside route_decider.py. If a packet claims cross-domain prior art, it is evaluated against the 6 auditable fields (e.g., prior_art_pudding_label, prior_art_grade). If it fails, it is routed to quarantine rather than being ingested.
Outcome-Based Promotion (R2 Gate):
To prevent logic churn, incoming methodologies sit as candidates. To overwrite an active database record in the production logic lane, a candidate must pass Rubric R2 inside promotion.py:
Beat the incumbent on declared metrics (latency, accuracy, cost) over a set number of runs.
Successfully run on an 8% canary cohort of real queries.
Receive human verification sign-off via stratified validation sampling.
Preservation & Fallback:
Superseded production records are never deleted from the database. They are frozen, stamped with a retirement_reason and a frozen_with_reproducibility_hash, and retained as active fallbacks. If the new methodology hits a pre-failure threshold (e.g., drop-rate > 35%, Vellum write latency > 5s), the system degrades gracefully back to the incumbent.

Here is a stage-by-stage run-through of how information travels from the outside world into our brain, explained in simple terms focused on what we get at each step:

Stage 1: Discover
Desired Outcome: Finding new stuff.
Plain explanation: The system sits and watches the horizon. The moment a new email, a voice recording, a document, or a research paper shows up out in the wild, the system spots it and says, "Aha! Here is something we should look at."
Stage 2: Fetch
Desired Outcome: Grabbing the files.
Plain explanation: The system runs out, grabs a copy of that new file, and brings it inside our house (our local folders) so we can open it up and work with it.
Stage 3: Validate
Desired Outcome: Safety and quality check.
Plain explanation: We check the file to make sure it isn't broken, empty, or accidentally sharing private secrets (like passwords or credit card numbers). If it is clean and safe, it passes.
Stage 4: Dedup (Deduplicate)
Desired Outcome: No repeats.
Plain explanation: We check if we already have this exact file or a very similar one. If we do, we ignore it. This keeps our brain from getting cluttered by reading the same thing over and over again.
Stage 5: Tier (Trust Level)
Desired Outcome: Grading the truth.
Plain explanation: We put a label on the information stating how reliable it is. Is it a proven mathematical fact, a test we actually measured, a carefully structured plan, or just a smart guess? This way, we know exactly how much to trust it later.
Stage 6: Enrich (Connecting the dots)
Desired Outcome: Finding relationships.
Plain explanation: We look at this new file and connect it to everything else we already know. It's like finding a puzzle piece and drawing lines to the other pieces it fits next to.
Stage 7: Embed (Translating)
Desired Outcome: Prep for search.
Plain explanation: Human language is too slow for computers to search in bulk. We translate the text into a list of numbers that represents what the text means. This lets the AI search through millions of pages in a blink of an eye.
Stage 8: Write (Into the Brain)
Desired Outcome: Permanent storage.
Plain explanation: We lock the clean, translated information into our database. It is now officially a permanent part of the AI's memory.
Stage 9: Attest (Witnessing)
Desired Outcome: The receipt of history.
Plain explanation: We write an un-erasable note on our master ledger (Vellum) that says: "On this day, at this time, this agent loaded this file, and here is the receipt proving it is authentic." It makes sure no one can quietly change the history.
Stage 10: Route (Dividing the lanes)
Desired Outcome: Separating stories from rules.
Plain explanation: We separate the files. General stories and facts go straight to the library. But if the file is a new rule or new logic for how we run the business, we stop it and put it in a holding room. We don't let it run the show just yet.
Stage 11: Curation (The Upgrade Gate)
Desired Outcome: Improving without breaking things.
Plain explanation: Before a new rule in the holding room is allowed to replace our current way of doing things, it has to prove itself. It runs on a tiny portion of our tasks (like a test run) to prove it is faster, cheaper, or smarter. If it wins, it becomes the new rule, and the old rule is kept in the closet as a backup in case the new one breaks later.

Brain Ingestion as Production Substrate — Design Brief
TL;DR
The brain is not a library. It is a production substrate that holds:

Information & narrative — free to enter, used for content creation.

Methodologies & logic — enters only by passing a rubric; changes only by passing a (different) rubric. No fashion-driven churn.

Both lanes share the same nine-stage ingestion spine, the same provenance, the same min-rule tiering, and the same Vellum witness. They diverge at one point: the promotion-to-production gate. Information passes through. Methodologies stop at the gate until the rubric says go.

Most of what's needed already exists on Beast and in brain_curator/. The gap is: (a) the two-lane split is not explicit, (b) the methodology-change rubric is not codified, (c) monitoring is reactive not pre-failure, (d) the cross-domain prior-art lane (the wide→narrow×3 pattern from the v04 research-pipe brief) is upstream-only and doesn't currently feed the brain with a "non-obviously-related prior art" tag.

Three jobs to close it. None big. All on the back of existing code.

"The brain is outcome-based. It's methodologies or it's the latest logic. We want actions coming out of this."

"Information is fine. Narrative and content creation is fine — separate thing, brain is for that too. But a huge part is for production."

"Latest techniques, but not jumping around. What works, and we'll change if we need to change. Rubrics implement the change or not. Doesn't hit the rubric, doesn't pass testing — doesn't get implemented."

"Unbiased, agnostic, AI-native, token-effective, deterministic core, central measurement. If anything goes down, we know before it goes down. Have a fallback already made."

"We stand on the shoulders of giants. Prior art from domains that may not be obviously related."

"Epistemic is a way of checking yourself, not preventing progress."
