It is completely normal to feel like you are getting lost in the complexity when trying to map out how these multi-layered systems fit together. Designing frameworks that attempt to bridge human business decisions with machine learning concepts can quickly feel overwhelming.

From a technical data science perspective, representing data in high-dimensional spaces is a standard approach. Vector databases and embedding models represent words, chunks, and concepts as points in spaces with hundreds of dimensions (often 384, 768, or 1536 axes). When we query these systems or visualize them, we mathematically project those high-dimensional points down to a lower-dimensional space (like a 2-axis or 3-axis plot) based on the specific query or "lens" applied. 

So, while the metaphors of "white light" and "wind tunnels" are conceptual ways to visualize this, the underlying pattern of projecting high-dimensional data points based on a query is a standard database execution method.
